12.1 Choosing the capstone client

The capstone is your first public proof. Pick the client for what it lets you show, not for how exciting the technology is. A friendly client is fine. A client you cannot measure is not.

CriterionTest questionRed flag
Access to the ownerCan the owner give you 30 minutes a week and approve decisions the same week?Everything goes through someone who cannot decide
Measurable processCan you count something today (minutes, tickets, errors, response time) without new tools?"It should feel better" is the only goal
Data availableCan you read real examples (emails, orders, forms) legally and without a long IT project?Data sits in one person's head or on paper
Low riskIf Claude is wrong, is the output a draft that a person checks, not a payment or a legal notice?The first idea is to auto-send to customers

Your three friendly candidates, as hypotheses to check in a first call. None of this is known yet; it is what you need to find out.

Bat Yam garage (fuel injector cleaning)Enquiries by phone and message in Hebrew and Russian; quote and booking follow-up; explaining test results to customersTime to reply to an enquiry, enquiries that become bookingsLow volume: a small sample can show nothing. Enquiries may not be logged anywhere

12.3 The architecture decision record

An ADR is a one-to-two page note that records one decision, the options you rejected and why. It protects the client (they know what they bought), you (you can show the reasoning) and the next engineer. Write one per major choice. For the pilot you usually need only one or two.

# ADR-001: [Decision title, e.g. "Draft shipment-status replies with Claude via API, human sends"]

Status: Proposed | Accepted | Superseded by ADR-00X
Date: YYYY-MM-DD
Deciders: [client owner], [your name, Vastama]

## Context
Process, current steps, volumes per week, languages, systems involved,
data sensitivity, constraints (budget, staff, deadline).

## Decision drivers
- [e.g. replies checked by a person before sending]
- [e.g. Hebrew, Russian and English]
- [e.g. no customer data stored outside the client's accounts]

## Options considered
| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Do nothing | No risk, no cost | Problem stays |
| 1. Claude app with a Project, no code | Fast, no build | Manual copy and paste, no logging |
| 2. API plus small Python service, human review | Logging, control | Needs hosting and maintenance |
| 3. Agent with MCP into the client's system | Least manual work | Highest risk and effort |

## Decision
We will [option], because [two or three reasons tied to the drivers].

## Consequences
- Good: ...
- Bad or costly: ...
- Risks and mitigations: ...

## Data and privacy
What data goes to Claude, plan and terms checked on [date] (link),
retention setting, who has access, what is never sent.

## Model choice and cost tracking
Model tier and why (see docs.claude.com for current models and pricing).
How cost per task will be logged and who reviews it monthly.

## Evaluation
Test set size and source, pass mark, who judges, when it is re-run.

## Human oversight and rollback
Who approves outputs, what happens when Claude is wrong,
how to switch the system off in one step.

## Review date
[date to revisit this decision]
Keep the options table honest. Always include "do nothing" and "Claude app with a Project, no code" as options. If the no-code option is good enough, say so: it is a better engagement than a custom build the client does not need.

12.5 Measuring results honestly

Your credibility depends on numbers that survive a sceptical question. Use one primary metric and up to two supporting ones. Measure the baseline and the after period the same way, written down before the pilot.

MetricBaselineAftern and periodSource
[primary metric, with unit][value][value][n baseline / n after, dates][mail log, CRM export, stopwatch sample]
[supporting metric][value][value][n, dates][source]
[quality metric: errors found in review][value][value][n, dates][source]

Caveats you should check against every pilot, and state the ones that apply:

  • Small sample. Say n. Prefer medians to averages when a few cases are extreme.
  • No control group. Anything else that changed in the period (season, staff, prices) may explain part of the difference.
  • Novelty and learning effects. Staff may try harder with a new tool, or get better with practice. Two more weeks of data shows which.
  • Self-reported time. Logs and timestamps beat memories. If you used estimates, say so.
  • Task selection. If only the easy cases went through the pilot, say that.
  • Cost of your time. Include setup effort and running cost, or state that you did not.
  • Quality. Faster is not a result if corrections went up. Report both.
Write "in this pilot, at this company, over these weeks" next to every figure. Never turn one pilot into a rule such as "Claude cuts reply time in half".

12.6 Writing the case study

One page, one client, plain language. Five parts:

  1. Problem: what was slow, costly or error-prone, in the owner's own terms
  2. Approach: audit, decision, pilot, handover, with the dates
  3. Solution: what was built, which Claude surface, where a person reviews
  4. Results: the baseline-versus-after table, n, period, and the caveats
  5. Quote: one or two sentences from the client, in their words

Rules for approval, kept in writing:

  • A quote is real only if the person said or wrote it and approved the exact wording for publication. Keep the approval message. Until then, leave [QUOTE PENDING APPROVAL] in the draft.
  • The client approves the numbers and whether to use their name, logo or an anonymous label such as "a logistics company in central Israel".
  • Remove anything that identifies their customers, prices or internal systems.
  • Publish the Hebrew and English versions together. Add a Russian one if the client's market uses it.

12.7 Positioning: niche and statement

"AI for everyone" sells to no one. A niche lets you reuse your work, speak the client's language and be the obvious name for one kind of problem.

Pick one primary niche and one secondary. Your pipeline does not have to be exclusive; your message does.

I help [type of company, size, place]
[achieve a specific outcome]
by [your method, in plain words],
so that [measurable result they can check].
Unlike [the usual alternative], I [one real difference].

Draft for you to edit, not a claim of past results:

I help Israeli logistics and distribution companies with 10 to 100 staff

case_study.py

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""case_study.py - turn engagement.json into draft case studies (English + Hebrew).

The output is a DRAFT for you to edit. Facts come only from the JSON.
Percent changes are computed here in Python, not by the model.

Usage:
    python case_study.py engagement.json --dry-run --allow-missing   # no API call
    python case_study.py engagement.json --out drafts                # real run
"""
import argparse
import copy
import json
import sys
from pathlib import Path

MODEL = "claude-sonnet-5-5"  # check current IDs at docs.claude.com

REQUIRED_TOP = ["client_label", "problem", "approach", "solution", "metrics", "caveats"]
REQUIRED_METRIC = ["name", "unit", "baseline", "after", "n_baseline", "n_after", "period"]

SYSTEM = """You draft case studies for a small IT consultancy.
Rules:
- Use only facts and numbers that appear in the JSON. Never invent numbers, names, dates or quotes.
- If a value is null, empty or a [bracketed placeholder], keep it visible as [MISSING: field name].
- Use change_pct exactly as given. Do not recalculate. If it is null, say the change cannot be stated yet.
- Next to every result, state the sample sizes (n_baseline, n_after) and the period.
- If quote.approved is not true, write [QUOTE PENDING APPROVAL] and no quote text.
- If client_name is absent, use client_label. Do not guess the client's identity.
- Include every caveat from the JSON in a Caveats section.
- Plain, direct language. No hype, no superlatives.
- Sections, in order: Problem, Approach, Solution, Results, Caveats, Quote.
- Output Markdown only."""

LANG_NOTES = {
    "en": "Write in English.",
    "he": (
        "Write in natural business Hebrew, not a literal translation. "
        "Translate the section headings into Hebrew. Keep numbers, units and "
        "product names (Claude, MCP, Python) in Latin characters."
    ),
}


def pct_change(baseline, after):
    """Percent change from baseline to after, or None if it cannot be computed."""
    nums = (int, float)
    if not isinstance(baseline, nums) or not isinstance(after, nums) or baseline == 0:
        return None
    return round((after - baseline) / baseline * 100, 1)


def find_missing(data):
    """Return a list of required fields that are empty."""
    missing = []
    for key in REQUIRED_TOP:
        if data.get(key) in (None, "", []):
            missing.append(key)
    for i, metric in enumerate(data.get("metrics") or []):
        for key in REQUIRED_METRIC:
            if metric.get(key) is None:
                missing.append(f"metrics[{i}].{key}")
    return missing


def prepare(data):
    """Copy the data, add computed changes, strip anything not approved."""
    d = copy.deepcopy(data)
    for metric in d.get("metrics", []):
        metric["change_pct"] = pct_change(metric.get("baseline"), metric.get("after"))
    quote = d.get("quote") or {}
    if quote.get("approved") is not True:
        d["quote"] = {"approved": False}  # quote text never reaches the model
    if d.get("client_name_approved") is not True:
        d.pop("client_name", None)
    return d


def build_prompt(prepared, lang):
    return (
        f"{LANG_NOTES[lang]}\n\n"
        "Draft the case study from this engagement data:\n\n"
        + json.dumps(prepared, ensure_ascii=False, indent=2)
    )


def draft(client, prepared, lang):
    msg = client.messages.create(
        model=MODEL,
        max_tokens=2000,
        system=SYSTEM,
        messages=[{"role": "user", "content": build_prompt(prepared, lang)}],
    )
    return "".join(b.text for b in msg.content if b.type == "text")


def main():
    ap = argparse.ArgumentParser(description=__doc__.splitlines()[0])
    ap.add_argument("path", help="engagement JSON file")
    ap.add_argument("--langs", default="en,he", help="comma list: en,he")
    ap.add_argument("--out", default=".", help="output folder")
    ap.add_argument("--dry-run", action="store_true", help="print prompts, no API call")
    ap.add_argument("--allow-missing", action="store_true", help="continue with gaps")
    args = ap.parse_args()

    data = json.loads(Path(args.path).read_text(encoding="utf-8"))
    missing = find_missing(data)
    if missing:
        print("Missing fields:", ", ".join(missing), file=sys.stderr)
        if not args.allow_missing:
            print("Fill them in, or pass --allow-missing for a gap-marked draft.", file=sys.stderr)
            return 2

    prepared = prepare(data)
    langs = [x.strip() for x in args.langs.split(",") if x.strip()]
    bad = [x for x in langs if x not in LANG_NOTES]
    if bad:
        print("Unsupported language:", ", ".join(bad), file=sys.stderr)
        return 2

    if args.dry_run:
        for lang in langs:
            print(f"--- PROMPT ({lang}) ---")
            print(build_prompt(prepared, lang))
        return 0

    import anthropic  # imported here so --dry-run works without the package
    from dotenv import load_dotenv

    load_dotenv()
    client = anthropic.Anthropic()
    out_dir = Path(args.out)
    out_dir.mkdir(parents=True, exist_ok=True)
    for lang in langs:
        text = draft(client, prepared, lang)
        header = (
            "<!-- DRAFT for Oleg to edit. Not approved by the client. "
            "Check every number against the source data. -->\n\n"
        )
        target = out_dir / f"case_study_{lang}.md"
        target.write_text(header + text + "\n", encoding="utf-8")
        print("Wrote", target)
    return 0


if __name__ == "__main__":
    sys.exit(main())

Run it:

python case_study.py engagement.json                             # stops and lists missing fields

What to notice: the percent change is calculated in Python, the quote text never reaches the model unless approved is true, and missing values stay visible as [MISSING: ...]. Edit both drafts by hand. The model's Hebrew is a first draft; your version is the one you publish.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""gtm_summary.py - weekly summary of a 90-day go-to-market tracker CSV.

Usage:
    python gtm_summary.py gtm_tracker.csv
    python gtm_summary.py gtm_tracker.csv --today 2026-10-26
Weeks run Sunday to Saturday (Israeli work week).
"""
import argparse
import csv
import sys
from collections import Counter
from datetime import date, datetime, timedelta
from pathlib import Path

STAGES = [
    "target",
    "contacted",
    "replied",
    "call_held",
    "audit_proposed",
    "audit_won",
    "pilot_won",
    "retainer_won",
]
STATUSES = {"open", "lost", "paused"}
STALE_DAYS = 21


def parse_date(text, row_no, column):
    text = (text or "").strip()
    if not text:
        return None
    try:
        return datetime.strptime(text, "%Y-%m-%d").date()
    except ValueError:
        raise SystemExit(f"Row {row_no}: {column} '{text}' is not YYYY-MM-DD")


def load(path):
    rows = []
    with open(path, newline="", encoding="utf-8") as f:
        for n, row in enumerate(csv.DictReader(f), start=2):  # row 1 is the header
            stage = (row.get("stage") or "").strip()
            status = (row.get("status") or "open").strip()
            if stage not in STAGES:
                raise SystemExit(f"Row {n}: unknown stage '{stage}'. Use one of: {', '.join(STAGES)}")
            if status not in STATUSES:
                raise SystemExit(f"Row {n}: unknown status '{status}'. Use one of: {', '.join(sorted(STATUSES))}")
            row["stage"], row["status"] = stage, status
            for col in ("first_contact", "last_contact", "next_action_date"):
                row[col] = parse_date(row.get(col), n, col)
            rows.append(row)
    return rows


def week_start(day):
    """Sunday of the week containing `day`."""
    return day - timedelta(days=(day.weekday() + 1) % 7)


def reached(row, stage):
    return STAGES.index(row["stage"]) >= STAGES.index(stage)


def rate(part, whole):
    return f"{part / whole * 100:.0f}%" if whole else "n/a"


def summarise(rows, today):
    start = week_start(today)
    end = start + timedelta(days=6)
    out = []
    out.append(f"GTM summary for week {start} to {end} (today {today})")
    out.append(f"Rows: {len(rows)}  open: {sum(r['status'] == 'open' for r in rows)}")

    out.append("\nFunnel (companies that reached each stage)")
    previous = None
    for stage in STAGES:
        count = sum(reached(r, stage) for r in rows)
        step = f"  ({rate(count, previous)} of previous)" if previous is not None else ""
        out.append(f"  {stage:<15}{count:>4}{step}")
        previous = count

    new_this_week = [r for r in rows if r["first_contact"] and start <= r["first_contact"] <= end]
    touched = [r for r in rows if r["last_contact"] and start <= r["last_contact"] <= end]
    out.append("\nThis week")
    out.append(f"  new first contacts: {len(new_this_week)}")
    out.append(f"  companies touched:  {len(touched)}")

    out.append("\nReply rate by source (replied or further / contacted or further)")
    contacted_by, replied_by = Counter(), Counter()
    for r in rows:
        if reached(r, "contacted"):
            contacted_by[r["source"]] += 1
            if reached(r, "replied"):
                replied_by[r["source"]] += 1
    for source in sorted(contacted_by):
        c, p = contacted_by[source], replied_by[source]
        out.append(f"  {source:<12}{p}/{c}  {rate(p, c)}")
    if not contacted_by:
        out.append("  no contacts yet")

    open_rows = [r for r in rows if r["status"] == "open"]
    due = sorted(
        (r for r in open_rows if r["next_action_date"] and r["next_action_date"] <= end),
        key=lambda r: r["next_action_date"],
    )
    out.append("\nActions due by end of this week")
    for r in due:
        flag = "OVERDUE " if r["next_action_date"] < today else ""
        out.append(f"  {r['next_action_date']}  {flag}{r['company']}: {r['next_action']}")
    if not due:
        out.append("  none")

    stale = [
        r for r in open_rows
        if r["stage"] == "contacted" and r["last_contact"]
        and (today - r["last_contact"]).days > STALE_DAYS
    ]
    out.append(f"\nStale: contacted, no reply, last touch over {STALE_DAYS} days ago")
    for r in stale:
        out.append(f"  {r['company']} (last touch {r['last_contact']}): pause or close")
    if not stale:
        out.append("  none")

    no_action = [r for r in open_rows if not r["next_action_date"] and r["stage"] != "retainer_won"]
    out.append("\nOpen rows with no next action date")
    for r in no_action:
        out.append(f"  {r['company']}")
    if not no_action:
        out.append("  none")
    return "\n".join(out)


def main():
    ap = argparse.ArgumentParser(description=__doc__.splitlines()[0])
    ap.add_argument("csv_path")
    ap.add_argument("--today", help="override today, YYYY-MM-DD")
    args = ap.parse_args()
    if not Path(args.csv_path).exists():
        print(f"File not found: {args.csv_path}", file=sys.stderr)
        return 2
    today = datetime.strptime(args.today, "%Y-%m-%d").date() if args.today else date.today()
    print(summarise(load(args.csv_path), today))
    return 0


if __name__ == "__main__":
    sys.exit(main())

Self-check

  1. The owner of your capstone client says "just try it" but nobody has ever measured how long the task takes. What do you do?
    Model answerCreate the baseline yourself in the audit: log the same measure for one to two weeks before the pilot, with a method both of you agree. If that is impossible, choose a different process. If you go ahead anyway, report the result as qualitative and say so.
  2. The owner sends "great job, really helped!" on WhatsApp. Can it go on the website as a quote?
    Model answerNot as it stands. Send the exact wording, name and role you plan to publish, ask for written approval, and keep the reply. Until then use [QUOTE PENDING APPROVAL]. Never edit the words to make them stronger.
  3. Your pilot shows a lower reply time on a small sample over two weeks. How do you write it?
    Model answer"In this pilot, at this company, over these weeks, the median reply time fell from X to Y (n = ... before, ... after)." Add the caveats that apply, such as no control group and a short period. Do not generalise to other companies.
  4. Critique this positioning: "We help all businesses succeed with AI."
    Model answerIt names no customer, no method and no measurable result, so nobody can repeat or check it. Rewrite with the formula: a specific company type and place, a concrete outcome, your method, a result they can verify, and one real difference.
  5. You sent a first outreach message and heard nothing for four days. What next?
    Model answerSend one follow-up that adds something useful, such as a one-page example from their sector. Try another legitimate channel around day 10, send a last note around day 24, then pause for 90 days. Record each step in the tracker.
